StayBERT: Staypoint Detection as the Pretext Task for Trajectory Foundation Models

Riyang Bao*, Mingke Tian*, Andreas Züfle, Liang Zhao

Emory University · * Equal contribution

Summary

Masked language modeling and masked image modeling gave text and vision a natural pretext task; trajectories have no equivalent. StayBERT proposes staypoint detection: deciding, point by point, whether a person is staying or moving requires reasoning about when movement pauses, where, for how long, and whether the surrounding geography fits a human activity. A motion view and a query-conditioned place view are fused by a dimension-wise gate and decoded by a CRF into coherent dwell spans. The frozen representation transfers from SF-LIFE to Atlanta and GeoLife across seven downstream tasks, and an optional CRF-only self-training step adapts it to a new city without labels.

Abstract

Trajectory foundation models lack a pretext task that naturally couples motion, time, continuous space, and geographic semantics. We propose staypoint detection as such a pretext: predicting dwell spans requires recognizing when movement pauses, where the pause occurs, how long it lasts, and whether the surrounding geography is compatible with human activity. We instantiate this idea as StayBERT, a motion–place dual-view trajectory foundation model. Its place stream uses a spherical coordinate query to compose local POIs, dual spatial-flow structure, and language-derived regional semantics; a vector-valued gate aligns this context with Transformer motion states before structured CRF decoding. Three of the four geographic sources are city-agnostic, enabling direct cross-region deployment after source-city pretraining. We pretrain StayBERT on a large-scale public mobility corpus and transfer its frozen representations to trajectory data from other cities through lightweight task-specific heads. Across seven downstream tasks spanning semantic classification, anomaly detection, duration regression, and sequential prediction, StayBERT demonstrates broad cross-dataset transfer. These results establish staypoint prediction as a scalable pretext for learning transferable trajectory representations that couple movement dynamics with geographic semantics.

1Motivation

Existing trajectory pretraining objectives capture only part of the interaction between movement and place. Coordinate reconstruction and masked recovery can exploit highly correlated neighboring points and reward smoothness rather than behavior. Masked prediction over road-segment sequences hides continuous geometry and ties the vocabulary to one network. Contrastive learning depends on augmentations and pair construction, and language-model approaches recast stays or check-ins as text rather than representing continuous movement.

A staypoint, a contiguous period during which a person remains within a region, has long been the unit for extracting significant places from GPS traces. Detecting one requires joint reasoning about spatial concentration, temporal persistence, motion transitions, and place compatibility: low speed may mean an activity, congestion, a traffic signal, or positioning noise, and only movement and environment together tell these apart. Using it as a pretext raises three challenges:

  1. Heterogeneous evidence. Movement dynamics must be integrated with continuous coordinates, nearby POIs, mobility structure, and regional semantics.
  2. Structured spans. A staypoint is a temporal span, so the representation must preserve continuous geometry, sparse arrival and departure boundaries, and complete dwell segments.
  3. Transfer across cities. Sampling rates, mobility patterns, and available geographic context differ by city, and the representation must remain useful when some context is missing.
Contributions
  • Staypoint detection formulated as a trajectory pretraining task that explicitly connects motion dynamics with geographic context and produces reusable dwell representations.
  • StayBERT, a motion–place dual-view framework with query-conditioned geographic composition and dwell-structured learning, together with Geo-TTST for lightweight cross-city adaptation.
  • An evaluation of frozen representations across seven downstream tasks and three trajectory datasets, with comparisons to public representation methods and targeted ablations.

2Task formulation

A GPS trajectory of \(N\) points is labeled point by point as stop or move:

\[\mathcal{T}=\{(\ell_i,t_i)\}_{i=1}^{N},\qquad \ell_i=(\mathrm{lat}_i,\mathrm{lon}_i),\qquad y_i\in\{\text{stop},\text{move}\}\]

A maximal contiguous span labeled stop defines a dwell episode, so the target captures both point-level movement evidence and the temporal coherence of the span. StayBERT learns a contextual state for every point, decodes a coherent stop/move sequence, and aggregates the states within each dwell into a reusable representation:

\[\mathcal{T}\ \longrightarrow\ (\mathbf{m}_i,\mathbf{g}_i)\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{h}_i\ \longrightarrow\ \mathrm{CRF}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbf{z}_s\]

3Motion–place encoder

Motion view: how is the person moving?

A temporal convolutional front-end produces a smoothed coordinate estimate. Each motion token combines it with the original coordinates, the elapsed time \(\Delta t_i\), and the log-speed, and a two-layer Transformer (\(d_{\mathrm{model}}=256\), eight heads) contextualizes the tokens into \(\mathbf{m}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{256}\). This stream captures velocity changes, temporal persistence, and the approach and departure patterns around dwell boundaries.

Place view: four levels of geographic evidence

  1. Geodesic anchor. Sphere2Vec maps longitude and latitude to spherical basis functions at 16 frequencies, projected to a 64-dimensional query \(\mathbf{q}_i=\Phi_{\mathrm{loc}}(\ell_i)\).
  2. Functional neighborhood. Up to \(K=20\) nearby POIs, each a venue-type embedding plus a multi-scale encoding of its displacement, summarized by attention from \(\mathbf{q}_i\) into \(\mathbf{c}^{\mathrm{poi}}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{64}\).
  3. Mobility structure. Two-layer GraphSAGE branches over a city grid, one on spatial adjacency and functional similarity and one on observed directed flows, projected to \(\mathbf{c}^{\mathrm{net}}_i\). This is the only city-specific source.
  4. Region semantics. Each grid cell’s POI composition and dominant land use, described in text, embedded with a frozen Sentence-BERT model, and reduced to 64 dimensions by PCA, giving \(\mathbf{c}^{\mathrm{sem}}_i\).

The four levels are composed rather than concatenated. The Sphere2Vec query attends over a context bank of the available tokens:

\[C_i=\big[\mathbf{c}^{\mathrm{poi}}_i;\ \mathbf{c}^{\mathrm{net}}_i;\ \mathbf{c}^{\mathrm{sem}}_i\big],\qquad \mathbf{g}_i=W_o\,\mathrm{LN}\big(\mathbf{q}_i+\mathrm{MHA}(\mathbf{q}_i,C_i,C_i)\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{128}\]

A missing source is omitted from \(C_i\) rather than replaced by a placeholder; with no external context, the place view reduces to the Sphere2Vec query.

Motion–place alignment

The two states are projected into a shared 128-dimensional space and combined by a point-wise, dimension-wise gate:

\[\tilde{\mathbf{m}}_i=W_m\mathbf{m}_i,\qquad \tilde{\mathbf{g}}_i=W_g\mathbf{g}_i,\qquad \boldsymbol{\alpha}_i=\sigma\big(W_\alpha[\mathbf{m}_i;\mathbf{g}_i]\big),\qquad \mathbf{h}_i=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_i\odot\tilde{\mathbf{m}}_i+(1-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_i)\odot\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_i\]

The gate balances evidence rather than forcing a global choice: some dimensions can respond to sharp motion changes while others retain stable functional context.

Structured decoding and staypoint pooling

A linear-chain CRF maps \(\{\mathbf{h}_i\}\) to stop/move emissions, learns transition compatibility, and uses Viterbi decoding to turn local evidence into coherent dwell intervals. For a staypoint interval \(s\), with \(p_i\) the CRF marginal probability of stop at point \(i\),

\[S_\theta(\mathcal{T},\mathbf{y})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_\theta(\mathbf{h}_i)_{y_i}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}A_{y_{i-1},y_i},\qquad w_i=\frac{p_i}{\sum_{j\in s}p_j},\qquad \mathbf{z}_s=\sum_{i\in s}w_i\mathbf{h}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{128}\]

The soft weighting emphasizes the points most characteristic of the dwell, and \(\mathbf{z}_s\) is the transferable representation that downstream heads consume.

4Dwell-structured pretraining objective

The primary term is a length-normalized, move-aware CRF negative log-likelihood, which keeps long stop interiors from overwhelming the shorter arrival and departure intervals:

\[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bal\text{-}CRF}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|}\sum_{k}\omega_k\,\frac{-\log P_\theta\big(\mathbf{y}^{(k)}\mid\mathcal{T}^{(k)}\big)}{N_k},\qquad \omega_k=1+(w_{\mathrm{move}}-1)\,r_k\]

where \(N_k\) is the valid length of trajectory \(k\), \(r_k\) its fraction of move points, and \(w_{\mathrm{move}}=20\). Three auxiliary terms act at complementary scales:

\[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dwell}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bal\text{-}CRF}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{rec}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{bnd}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bnd}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{seg}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{seg}}\]
\[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}}\big\lVert d_\psi(\mathbf{h}_i)-\bar{\ell}_i\big\rVert_2^2,\qquad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bnd}}=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}}\log P_\theta(y_i\mid\mathcal{T})_i,\qquad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{seg}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\Big[\lVert\mu^{p}_s-\mu_s\rVert_1+\beta\,\bar{p}_{\partial s}\Big]\]

where

\(\mathcal{M}\)
masked valid positions, whose normalized coordinates are reconstructed
\(\mathcal{B}\)
labeled arrival and departure transitions, \(\{i:y_i\neq y_{i-1}\}\)
\(\mathcal{S}\)
ground-truth dwell spans; \(\mu^p_s\) is the stop-probability-weighted centroid of predicted coordinates and \(\mu_s\) the geometric centroid
\(\bar{p}_{\partial s}\)
the mean stop probability just outside the span, which penalizes leakage beyond its boundaries

Before all terms, adaptive trajectory resampling (ATR) randomly thins each trajectory while preserving order, so the same ten-minute dwell may be observed every 10 or every 30 seconds. It changes how a trajectory is observed rather than adding a loss, and discourages dependence on one sampling rate.

5Cross-city adaptation: Geo-TTST

After pretraining, StayBERT can be deployed to a target city without target labels. Sphere2Vec, POI attention, and language semantics share a common cross-city interface, and the city-specific graph token is omitted when no compatible target graph exists. This is direct transfer. When an unlabeled target corpus is available, Geo-Conditional Test-Time Self-Training adds an optional adaptation step.

Predictive confidence alone can be unreliable under geographic shift, so the gate supplies a second reliability signal, the model’s reliance on the place stream:

\[c_i=\gamma\,p^\star_i+(1-\gamma)\,a^{\mathrm{geo}}_i,\qquad p^\star_i=\max_y P(y\mid\mathcal{T})_i,\qquad a^{\mathrm{geo}}_i=\operatorname{mean}_d\big(1-\alpha_{i,d}\big)\]

Only points with \(c_i>\tau\) contribute pseudo-labels \(\hat{y}_i=\arg\max_y P(y\mid\mathcal{T})_i\), which are re-extracted each round with the latest model. Within a round, the encoder, place encoder, and fusion stay frozen, and only the CRF parameters are updated on the selected points:

\[\mathcal{L}^{(r)}_{\mathrm{TTST}}=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}^{(r)}|}\sum_{(j,i)\in\mathcal{I}^{(r)}}\log p_{\theta_{\mathrm{CRF}}}\big(\hat{y}^{(r)}_{j,i}\mid\mathcal{T}_j\big)_i\]

Because confidence filtering can keep non-contiguous points, adaptation uses the differentiable CRF marginals at the selected points rather than a full-sequence likelihood. Updating only the CRF head recalibrates stop/move decoding without overwriting the generic representation \(\mathbf{h}_i\). Target stop/move labels are never used.

6Experiments

Datasets. Pretraining uses 1,000 agents from the public 500K-agent SF-LIFE corpus, resampled to 10 s. Transfer targets are 500 Atlanta agents with about 22M GPS points, and a GeoLife subset of 177 users with 19,372 derived staypoints. All splits are agent-disjoint 70/15/15, and Atlanta and GeoLife place labels are mapped to SF-LIFE’s six venue classes. Tasks. Frozen embeddings feed task-specific heads for Home/Work (three-class macro F1), Activity (six-class macro F1), Anomaly detection (AUROC), Duration forecasting (MAE in minutes), NextActivity (macro F1), NextLocation (Top-1, Top-5, Acc@500 m, Acc@1 km), and Trajectory User Linking. Training. AdamW, batch 128, at most 30 epochs with early stopping, and a 20-epoch warm-up that gradually admits the geographic context.

  • 0.9990staypoint segment F1 on the SF-LIFE test split (pretext sanity)
  • 0.7109NextActivity F1 on Atlanta after Geo-TTST, against 0.6599 for a model trained in Atlanta
  • 0.7256Home/Work F1 transferred to Atlanta without any parameter update

The full model gives the strongest semantic transfer and the best embedding-only NextLocation Top-1 and 1 km accuracy. Removing POI context degrades every metric and roughly doubles Duration MAE, from 80.8 to 166.7 minutes. The ablations reveal task-specific trade-offs among the geographic sources rather than a single ordering.

8Conclusion

StayBERT casts staypoint detection as a dwell-centric pretext and learns transferable motion–place representations through geographic context alignment and structured decoding. Across SF-LIFE, Atlanta, and GeoLife, frozen embeddings support semantic classification, duration forecasting, anomaly detection, and sequential prediction, while target features and CRF-only Geo-TTST provide selective gains. Task-specific retrieval, personal routines, dataset differences, and mobility privacy remain important directions for future work.
