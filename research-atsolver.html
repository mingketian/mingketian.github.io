1Problem and significance

A workload consists of \(K\) sparse linear systems that share one operator,

\[A x_k=b_k,\quad k=1,\dots,K,\qquad A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\ \text{sparse},\qquad B_{\mathrm{rhs}}=[b_1,\dots,b_K]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times K},\]

with \(K=1\) in the single-system regime \(\mathsf{S}\) and \(K>1\) in the batch regime \(\mathsf{M}\). A third regime \(\mathsf{Q}\) is a sequence of related systems whose operator may change between solves. The workload contract \(\mathcal{W}=(L,R,H,B)\) fixes the regime and system characteristics \(L\), the accuracy and coverage requirements \(R\), the hardware and solver capabilities \(H\), and the resource and search budgets \(B\). A configuration \(a\) chooses a solver, a preconditioner, their numerical parameters, and permitted preprocessing and setup-reuse operations.

Acceptance is decided only by recomputing each residual on the original system:

\[\begin{aligned}\rho_k(a)&=\frac{\lVert A\hat{x}_k(a)-b_k\rVert_2}{\max\{\lVert b_k\rVert_2,\ \epsilon_b\}},\\ \mathrm{acc}(a)&=\mathbf{1}\big\{\rho_k(a)\le\tau_k\ \ \forall k\big\}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{\text{finite, valid, within time and memory limits}\},\end{aligned}\]

where \(\epsilon_b>0\) guards near-zero right-hand sides. The cost of using a configuration on the workload, excluding the search that found it, is

\[C_{\mathrm{deploy}}(a;K)=C_{\mathrm{pre}}(a)+C_{\mathrm{setup}}(a)+\sum_{k=1}^{K}C_{\mathrm{solve},k}(a)+C_{\mathrm{transition}}(a).\]

The configuration problem is therefore feasibility-first and budgeted: within at most \(B_{\mathrm{prop}}\) proposals and \(B_{\mathrm{exec}}\) executions, find an accepted configuration, then prefer the cheapest one,

\[a^\star\in\arg\min_{a\,:\,\mathrm{acc}(a)=1}\ C_{\mathrm{deploy}}(a;K)\qquad\text{subject to}\quad \#\text{proposals}\le B_{\mathrm{prop}},\ \ \#\text{executions}\le B_{\mathrm{exec}}.\]
  • The objective depends on the workload. The \(C_{\mathrm{setup}}\) term in Eq. (3) is paid once while the solve term grows with \(K\), so an expensive setup that is wasteful for one solve can be optimal for a batch.
  • The quantities that matter are observed only after execution. Convergence, the achieved residual, phase timings, and memory are revealed by running a candidate. Configuration is a sequential decision in which each trial is evidence for the next.
  • Correctness cannot be delegated. A learned proposer may be wrong or may claim success. Acceptance in Eq. (2) must stay with trusted numerical software, and every proposal, including invalid, repeated, or failed ones, must be charged to the budget.

2Research questions

The three properties above define what a controller must do: use execution evidence to revise proposals within a fixed budget, retain useful information across trials and searches, and adapt the setup–solve trade-off to the workload. A fourth question concerns the input representation of the matrix.

3Method

ATSolver separates proposal from authority. A pretrained language model \(\pi_\theta\) with fixed parameters chooses what to try; numerical software decides whether a proposal is legal, executes it, verifies the result, charges its cost, and keeps the best verified configuration. Algorithm 1 gives one search.

Algorithm 1  ATSolver search for one workload

Input: contract \(\mathcal{W}\); limits \(B_{\mathrm{prop}},B_{\mathrm{exec}}\); policy \(\pi_\theta\) (fixed).  Output: verified incumbent \(a^\star\) or a reasoned failure.

  1. build the workload card \(c\) from \(A\), \(B_{\mathrm{rhs}}\), \(\mathcal{W}\); charge \(C_{\mathrm{card}}\)  ▷ names and paths withheld
  2. \(a^\star\leftarrow\varnothing\);  \(m\leftarrow\varnothing\);  \(C_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\leftarrow C_{\mathrm{card}}\)
  3. for \(j=1,\dots,B_{\mathrm{prop}}\):
  4. \(d_j\leftarrow(c,\ \mathrm{proj}(m),\ \mathcal{A}_j,\ \beta_j)\);  \(a_j\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid d_j)\)  ▷ Eq. (5)
  5. if \(a_j=\textsf{STOP}\) and the stop rule admits it: break
  6. \(\eta_j\leftarrow\mathrm{gate}(a_j,\mathcal{A}_j)\); if rejected: \(m\leftarrow m\cup\{(a_j,\eta_j,\delta_j)\}\); continue  ▷ still charged to \(B_{\mathrm{prop}}\)
  7. execute \(a_j\) from the prescribed numerical state; observe \(e_j\); \(C_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\leftarrow C_{\mathrm{ctrl}}+C^{\mathrm{num}}_j\)  ▷ Eq. (6)
  8. verify every residual (Eq. 2); if failed: \(\delta_j\leftarrow\mathrm{diagnose}(e_j)\)
  9. update \(a^\star\) by the replacement rule (Eq. 7); \(m\leftarrow m\cup\{(a_j,e_j,\delta_j)\}\); stop if \(B_{\mathrm{exec}}\) is exhausted
  10. return \(a^\star\), the full ledger \(m\), and \(C_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\)

3.1Decision state and policy

Before proposal \(j\), the model sees the workload information \(c_j\), the trial history \(m^{\mathrm{arm}}_j\) permitted by the experimental condition, the legal action set \(\mathcal{A}_j\), and the remaining budgets \(\beta_j\):

\[d_j=\big(c_j,\ m^{\mathrm{arm}}_j,\ \mathcal{A}_j,\ \beta_j\big),\qquad a_j\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid d_j).\]

The card \(c_j\) contains the dimension \(n\), \(\mathrm{nnz}\), structural and scaling properties, trusted symmetry and SPD evidence, the accuracy and resource requirements, and the available solvers. Where enabled, it is extended by matrix views (sparsity in natural and reverse Cuthill–McKee order, positive and negative magnitudes) and by retrieved records. Matrix names, file paths, private numerical objects, and verifier state are withheld. The same \(\theta\) is used for the first proposal and for every revision; adaptation happens through \(d_j\), not through parameter updates.

3.2Typed action space

An action is a complete typed configuration over a fixed portfolio. The model does not write solver code; each proposal must contain exactly the required fields of its branch, and trusted checks restrict which branches are legal for a given matrix.

3.3Gate, execution, and search cost

Every submission counts toward \(B_{\mathrm{prop}}\), including invalid, duplicate, unaffordable, and premature \(\textsf{STOP}\) proposals. Only proposals that pass the schema and legality gate are executed and count toward \(B_{\mathrm{exec}}\). With \(g_j=1\) if proposal \(j\) is executed, total numerical search work is

\[C_{\mathrm{ctrl}}=C_{\mathrm{card}}+\sum_{j:\,g_j=1}C^{\mathrm{num}}_j ,\]

where \(C^{\mathrm{num}}_j\) covers preprocessing, setup, solves, verification, and permitted state transfer, for successful and failed trials alike. Work to first acceptance, \(C_{\mathrm{first}}\), sums the same terms up to the first accepted trial and is infinite if none is accepted. Model and orchestration latency are reported separately as full search time.

3.4Verification and the incumbent

An executed trial returns its acceptance status, residuals, phase timings, and memory; a rejected proposal returns its rejection reason. After a failure, a deterministic diagnostic summarizes the failure type and the aspects that may need revision; it does not choose the next solver. Software keeps the best accepted configuration under a fixed study objective \(J\):

\[a^\star_j=\begin{cases}a_j & \mathrm{acc}(a_j)=1\ \text{and}\ J(a_j)<(1-\kappa)\,J(a^\star_{j-1}),\\ a^\star_{j-1} & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\]

with \(J=C_{\mathrm{deploy}}\) and \(\kappa=0\) (strict improvement) in the three-proposal and batch studies, and \(J=\) solve time with \(\kappa=0.05\) in the six-proposal, image, and retrieval studies. Later failures cannot remove an incumbent. Each candidate starts from the same prescribed numerical state: trial history is shared, but setup objects and iterates are not.

3.5Memory: history projections and retrieved experience (RQ2)

The experimental condition fixes the projection \(\mathrm{proj}(m)\) of the ledger that enters \(d_j\). Four within-search projections form a ladder:

\[\mathcal{H}_0:\ \varnothing\ \ \subset\ \ \mathcal{H}_1:\ \{a_i\}_{i<j}\ \ \subset\ \ \mathcal{H}_2:\ \{(a_i,\ \text{scalar outcome}_i)\}_{i<j}\ \ \subset\ \ \mathcal{H}_3:\ \{(a_i,\ e_i,\ \delta_i)\}_{i<j},\]

that is, no action history, tried configurations, configurations with scalar outcomes, and configurations with the structured verifier record. All four keep the shared diagnostic handoff after a failure. Across searches, the card can be extended by solver knowledge \(\mathsf{K}\) alone or by knowledge plus retrieved trial records from earlier workloads, \(\mathsf{K}{+}\mathsf{E}\); retrieved choices must still pass the current verifier.

3.6Batches and operator sequences (RQ3)

In \(\mathsf{M}\), a candidate must solve the entire batch, and solves from different candidates cannot be combined. Within one candidate a setup is built once and reused, so the per-right-hand-side cost is

\[\frac{C_{\mathrm{deploy}}(a;K)}{K}=\frac{C_{\mathrm{pre}}(a)+C_{\mathrm{setup}}(a)+C_{\mathrm{transition}}(a)}{K}+\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}C_{\mathrm{solve},k}(a),\]

and the optimum of Eq. (4) shifts toward configurations with larger setup and smaller per-solve cost as \(K\) grows, for example from a Krylov method with a light preconditioner to a sparse direct factorization. The batch size is part of \(c_j\), so the controller can act on this trade-off. In \(\mathsf{Q}\), a sequence of four systems with operator shifts is solved under one 7/7 budget, and only the selected accepted candidate may pass validated numerical state (setup, iterate) to the next system, subject to checks when the matrix changes.

4Experimental protocol

The benchmark has 35 sparse matrices from 32 families: 29 from SuiteSparse and 6 assembled with MFEM. Three model repetitions per workload give 105 runs per condition. The proposal model is gpt-5.6-terra; the numerical stack is HYPRE 3.1 and serial SuperLU 7. Unless stated otherwise, \(\tau=10^{-8}\) with full coverage, a 30 s per-action timeout, a 60 s worker deadline, and a 1 GiB memory cap.

6Ongoing extension: closed-loop agent reinforcement learning

The results above keep the proposal model fixed: ATSolver improves within a search through its decision state, never through parameter updates, and every revision costs another round of language-model reasoning. The extension in progress asks a different question: can specialist language agents, trained with parametric reinforcement learning, generate feedback-dependent executable control programs, and learn when such a program should keep running, when it should return control to the team, how it should be revised, and when it can be reused across tasks? The aim is to remove repeated reasoning and wasted executions without relaxing any numerical acceptance requirement. Scope: the single-system regime \(\mathsf{S}\), right-hand-side batches \(\mathsf{M}\), related-system sequences \(\mathsf{Q}\), and structured linear problems (scaling and reordering, block systems, null spaces and gauges). PDE formulation and discretization search are outside this study.

Status. The method, interfaces, training semantics, and evaluation protocol are specified, and a reference interpreter and the return and PPO loss functions pass protocol tests. No reinforcement-learning training has been run, and no result is claimed here.

6.1From the next configuration to a closed-loop program

Current agent policies, including ATSolver, emit one action per decision:

\[c_t\sim\pi_\theta\big(c\mid H_t,\mathcal{W}\big),\]

so every new observation triggers another call to the team. The extension makes the unit of decision a short program with feedback branches, generated by the team \(\Gamma_\Theta\) from the history \(H_j\), the contract \(\mathcal{W}\), and the knowledge state \(\mathcal{K}_j\):

\[\omega_j\sim\Gamma_\Theta\big(\cdot\mid H_j,\mathcal{W},\mathcal{K}_j\big),\qquad \omega=\big(\mathcal{I}_\omega,\ G_\omega,\ \beta_\omega,\ \mathcal{P}_\omega\big).\]

This changes three things. The horizon of a decision: one generation produces an observe–test–call–continue-or-return loop rather than a single tool action. The use of test-time compute: feedback that the program already anticipates is handled by a deterministic interpreter, and language reasoning is spent only when the program's conditions no longer hold. Programs that are too short call the model constantly; programs that are too long or too permissive keep executing after conditions change; programs that are too conservative return at every change and gain nothing from reuse. The aim is to learn the boundary between these regimes. The role of knowledge: the library stores control relations that can be rebound in a new task (which observations justify continuing, which require re-examination, which fields change together), not only configurations that worked once.

6.3Formulation

The numerical task and acceptance rule are those of Eqs. (1)–(2). The action space is the complete legal domain exported by the implemented adapters, not a fixed grid of recipes:

\[\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{num}}(\mathcal{W},\xi)=\bigcup_{b\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{impl}}}\big\{(b,\nu,\ell):\ \nu\in\mathcal{D}_b,\ \mathrm{Legal}(\mathcal{W},\xi,b,\nu,\ell)=1\big\},\]

where \(\xi\) is the private numerical state, \(b\) a backend branch, \(\nu\) its declared fields, and \(\ell\) a supported lifecycle operation. Training uses task groups \(\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{W}_1,\dots,\mathcal{W}_N)\) with weights fixed inside a group; independent tasks communicate only through a bounded knowledge state \(\mathcal{K}_i\). The objective is constrained:

\[\max_{\Theta}\ \mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{N}Y_i\Big]\qquad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathbb{E}[C_j]\le B_j,\qquad \mathrm{len}(\mathcal{K}_i)\le B_K,\]

with \(Y_i\) the full-task success indicator and \(C_j\) each measured cost (model, tools, processor). Two objects are kept separate. The language policy \(\pi_{\theta_r}\) of role \(r\) is a trainable model that emits queries, complete numerical actions, programs, revisions, and memory operations. The program \(\omega\) is a discrete product of one generation; its deterministic interpretation has no gradient and produces no new token probabilities.

Table 16. Components of a program \(\omega\) and who produces them.
ComponentMeaningProduced by
\(\mathcal{I}_\omega\)Entry conditions: the observable situations in which the program appliesAnalysis, Diagnosis, knowledge selection
\(G_\omega\)Bounded control graph of tool calls, observation branches, and local stateRecommendation and Fixing
\(\beta_\omega\)RETURN and FINISH paths: when to continue, stop, or hand control backGenerated conditions, learned from execution return
\(\mathcal{P}_\omega\)Explicit parameter bindings and the required catalog versionProposed by roles, checked by a fixed schema

"Learning a termination condition" therefore means the following. Diagnosis may generate last.iterations > threshold → RETURN(Diagnosis); the threshold, comparison, and target role are generated tokens, and the return realized after execution changes the probability of generating such conditions. When the interpreter evaluates the same test again it does not resample, so the log-probability is counted once, at generation. A direct complete proposal remains available at all times, either directly or as the one-step program CALL → FINISH, so the program language never removes numerical capability.

6.4Program language and execution semantics

Programs are typed JSON control graphs checked against the live capability catalog. Arbitrary code, file access, network access, imports, and recursive model calls are not allowed.

Table 17. Node types. A program has at most 16 nodes, 8 complete actions, and 64 KiB; one execution visits at most 64 nodes, and a task at most 256.
NodeFieldsSemantics
TESTfield, cmp, value, then, else, missingReads a permitted public scalar and branches three ways; null, NaN, Inf, or a type mismatch takes the missing branch
OBSERVEfeature, nextAcquires a whitelisted diagnostic at its real cost; cannot write acceptance fields
CALLaction, nextSubmits a complete action; re-gated against the current state, executed, and verified on the original system
ADVANCEnext\(\mathsf{Q}\) only: commits the selected accepted state and exposes the next system
RETURNrole, reasonStops the interpreter and hands control to the named specialist role
FINISHstatusRequests delivery; success is decided by the trusted layer

Compilation checks, in order: strict JSON, top-level fields, size limits, catalog hash, the static schema of every action, the type of every tested field, edge validity, reachability of an exit from every node, and a content hash. Nothing is silently corrected. Each CALL inside a program counts as one proposal and, if admitted, one execution, so wrapping three calls in a program does not make them cheaper. Four kinds of exit are recorded separately, and only the first is a learned performance boundary: a generated RETURN condition, a missing-value branch, a forced return on catalog or state incompatibility, and budget or fuel exhaustion.

Algorithm 2  Example program for a \(\mathsf{Q}\) sequence (the threshold is a protocol fixture, not a tuned value)

  2. state: if a committed public state exists: CALL(complete reuse action); else CALL(complete build action)  ▷ the gate still checks compatibility

6.6Reward and policy optimization

The problem is cooperative and partially observable: the global state includes the workload, public history, knowledge, program stack, and private numerical state, while role \(r\) sees only its local history \(h^{(r)}\). In the default feasibility-first profile, the task reward is paid once at first delivery and every event pays its measured cost:

\[r_t=\mathbf{1}\{\text{task } i \text{ first delivered at } t\}\,Y_i-\lambda_n\frac{\Delta C_{n,t}}{B_{n,i}}-\lambda_m\frac{\Delta C_{m,t}}{B_{m,i}},\]

with numerical cost \(C_n\) and model cost \(C_m\) normalized by their budgets. A refinement profile adds a bounded deployment-quality term for accepted deliveries, with \(C_{0,i}\) and \(\eta\) frozen on development data:

\[U_i=Y_i\Big[1+\eta\,\frac{C_{0,i}}{C_{0,i}+C_{\mathrm{deploy},i}}\Big].\]

No reward is given for generating a program, reusing one, or calling the model less; those are outcomes to be measured, not incentives. Index every real language-model output by \(k\) with role \(r_k\), and let \(\bar{r}_k\) collect all costs and rewards from that output until the next one, including every tool step of a program it launched. With \(\gamma=1\),

\[G_k=\sum_{j=k}^{K-1}\bar{r}_j,\qquad A_k=G_k-V_{\phi_{\mathrm{old}}}(\chi_k),\]

where the critic input \(\chi_k\) contains only the joint public record, role, and budget before sampling. The trajectory probability factorizes over the roles that actually generated tokens, giving the likelihood-ratio gradient

\[p_\Theta(\tau)=p_{\mathrm{env}}(\tau)\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}\pi_{\theta_{r_k}}\big(y_k\mid h^{(r_k)}_k\big),\qquad \nabla_{\theta_r}J=\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{k:\,r_k=r}\nabla_{\theta_r}\log\pi_{\theta_r}\big(y_k\mid h^{(r)}_k\big)\,A_k\Big].\]

A program segment of \(\Delta\) tool events is a temporally extended action in the semi-Markov sense; its value includes the in-segment cost and the value after return. Training uses token-level PPO on the generated tokens only:

\[\rho_{k\ell}(\Theta)=\exp\!\big[\log\pi_\Theta(y_{k\ell}\mid h_k,y_{k,<\ell})-\log\pi_{\mathrm{old}}(y_{k\ell}\mid h_k,y_{k,<\ell})\big],\]
\[L_{\mathrm{actor}}=-\frac{1}{N_g}\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathrm{gen}}\min\big\{\rho_{k\ell}A_k,\ \mathrm{clip}(\rho_{k\ell},1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A_k\big\},\qquad L_V=\frac{1}{N_e}\sum_k\big(V_\phi(\chi_k)-G_k\big)^2,\]

where \(N_g\) is the number of task groups. The actor mask is 1 only for tokens the role itself generated (programs, conditions, parameters, queries, messages, knowledge updates) and 0 for prompts, other roles' messages, tool receipts, and processor output. Compiler-copied fields and deterministic branch decisions carry no token probability, and a program retrieved from the library is input: only the current selection, binding, and revision are trained. Initial settings: shared base weights with role LoRA adapters (rank 16, \(\alpha=32\)), actor and critic learning rates \(10^{-5}\) and \(5\times10^{-5}\), clip \(\epsilon=0.2\), one PPO epoch, Monte Carlo returns, temperature 1, top-\(p\) 1.

Algorithm 3  One training update
  1. freeze all role parameters and record the behaviour manifest (weights, tokenizer, sampling settings)
  2. sample training task groups; reset each group to the same initial knowledge snapshot
  3. run the team and its programs task by task, with weights fixed inside the group:
  4. store generated tokens, old log-probabilities, and the role mask; append an immutable cost receipt for every tool event
  5. place the task reward at delivery; a later memory write is credited only with return after it
  6. accumulate returns backward over each group (Eq. 16) and compute advantages with the old critic
  7. apply one PPO epoch and the value update (Eqs. 18–19); synchronize all rollout workers

6.7Program knowledge

Three layers are kept apart: an immutable fact store of every configuration and receipt, written by software; an editable program library of control-graph templates with binding schemas, public applicability conditions, evidence, limitations, and catalog compatibility; and fixed domain documentation shared by all methods. Numerical handles, solution vectors, test answers, and future tasks never enter the library. Recommendation and Diagnosis learn to select or skip a template, bind its parameters explicitly, and revise it; Reflection learns add, update, delete, or no-op, with one persistent commit per task, a 4096-token library budget, and at most 1024 tokens per retrieval. A template is called only after every binding has been generated and type-checked; a stale catalog or incompatible condition returns to the role instead of being migrated silently. Transfer is evaluated under two separate protocols: weights and library frozen, and weights frozen with the library updated from public feedback.

6.9Evaluation plan

Data are split by matrix root before generating right-hand sides, parameters, budgets, and orders, so variants of one root never cross splits; family hold-out and size extrapolation are reported separately. The pilot uses about 100/30/60 train/dev/test roots across \(\mathsf{S}\), \(\mathsf{M}\) (\(K\in\{2,8,32\}\)), \(\mathsf{Q}\) (smooth, abrupt, sparsity-pattern, and dimension changes), and structured systems. Task groups start at \(N=1\) and grow to \(N=3\), mixing one stationary related task, one related task with changed conditions, and one unrelated control.

The primary metric is success within budget \(\mathbf{B}\), reported per regime with micro and macro averages, together with the minimum budget that reaches a success level fixed on development data. The cost of first success counts failures through a restricted mean:

\[\widehat{S}_\Pi(\mathbf{B})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_i(\mathbf{B}),\qquad \mathrm{RMW}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\min\big(T^{\mathrm{first}}_i,T_{\max}\big).\]

Mechanism measures (model-free tool steps per language call, learned RETURN count, forced interruptions, recovery cost after failure, setup and rebuild counts, tasks rescued or broken by knowledge) are reported with success and full cost, never as rewards. Ablations cover fixed sequences against conditional branches, fixed against learned returns, always-step-wise reasoning against delegated execution, frozen, updated, deleted, or mismatched knowledge, budget sweeps of one checkpoint, IPPO against a centralized critic, and unseen families, sizes, and resource levels. Pre-registered comparisons are B5−B2, B5−B3, and B5−B0, with at least three training seeds in the pilot. Implementation proceeds through gated stages: interface manifests, the interpreter, real numerical tests for every regime, supervised and step-wise RL, program RL, knowledge RL, and finally the full experiments. If fixed programs with standard RL perform as well, or the cross-task gain disappears under full accounting, the corresponding claim is withdrawn.
